Television Energy Tips

Screen size and screen technology both affect how much power a TV uses. Larger screens and older types like plasma use more electricity.

Eco mode

Eco mode is a power-saving setting that can reduces electricity consumption by reducing screen brightness and backlight intensity.

Voice features

Turn off always-on voice listening if you don't use it - it keeps drawing power in standby.

Energy label

Check the star rating and yearly kWh on the energy label before purchasing.

Quick fact: Bigger screens use more power. A 16-inch TV draws around 14.7 W, a 32-inch TV around 15–42 W, a 55-inch TV around 45–223 W, and the largest 116-inch TV reaches about 355 W. The wide range within each size shows that two TVs of the same size can use very different amounts of power.

TV Energy Insights: Data Story

This section presents the Australian Government’s Television Energy Rating data to show which technologies, screen sizes and brands dominate the market, and how they relate to power use and efficiency. The aim is to help Australian consumers pick a TV that suits their home.

Chart 1: What type of TV screen technologies are currently available in Australia and which are the most frequent?

LCD (LED), LCD and OLED are currently available in Australia and LCD(LED) is the most common.

Chart 2: What screen sizes are available and which are most frequent?

Australian TVs range from small 16-inch models up to 116-inch screens. 55-inch (the tall red bar) and 65-inch (the tall green bar) which are more frequent than any other size.

Chart 3: Which brands have the largest number of models?

Kogan, LG and Samsung Electronics have the largest number of TV models on the Australian market.

Chart 4: Which type of screen technology consumes the least amount of power?

LCD TV's consume the least power, while OLEDs use slightly more but offer higher performance.

Chart 5: What is the relationship between screen size and power use?

There is a clear positive relationship between sscreen size and power use: larger TVs consume more electricity.

Chart 6: What is the relationship between star rating and screen size?

There is no clear relationship between screen size and star rating. TVs of every size show a wide range of star ratings, from about 1 to 9 stars. this means a large TV can still be highly efficient if it's well designed.

Chart 7: Are there differences in power consumption between brands?

Yes, power consumption differs between brands. TCL shows the highest outliers (600W), followed by LG. Most other brands cluster between 50 and 200W with tighter spreads. However, because every brand sells both efficient and inefficient models, the star rating on the label is a better guide than the brand name.
